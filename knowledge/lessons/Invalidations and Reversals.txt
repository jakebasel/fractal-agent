Invalidations & Reversals (course video, 24:09)

FVG invalidation. A fair value gap is invalidated when a candle closes above or below the leg that created that FVG and the following candle fails to rebalance price back into that range. Then we wait for a new valid FVG. If price only sweeps beyond the leg (a wick, or even a candle close) and the next candle re-enters and rebalances back into the original range, the FVG is not invalidated: that's a stop hunt, removing liquidity before continuing in the original direction. You can only invalidate an FVG on the same timeframe: a one minute FVG needs a one minute candle to close below the one minute leg; a 30-second needs a 30-second close.

Leg definition. Candles going in the same direction without taking out the previous candle's high or low. Going up, each candle cannot take out the previous candle's low (wicks count). Going down, each candle cannot take out the previous candle's high. A candle printed entirely inside the previous candle's range is in its shadow and doesn't count; the next candle out of the shadow decides. The moment a candle's wick takes out the previous candle's high (in a down leg), a new leg starts.

Bitcoin 1m example: first presented 1m FVG, tapped, first 30-second FVG. The market wicked the 30-second leg but did not close under it: a stop hunt, immediate rebalance, still valid; closure above the line was the entry (worked out to 1:2.36, roll at 2R). Had a 30-second candle closed under the line and the next candle not rebalanced, the 30-second is invalidated and we'd use the next 30-second FVG (a bigger one), and that becomes the new low.

One minute reversal / potential reversal. We look at two fractals at once. A double break on the one minute is technically still just an M on the five minute. Is the one minute strategy going to play out (DB, retracement, continuation) or the five minute strategy (manipulation, five-minute FVG entry the other way)? Three factors reduce the 80% directional bias after a 1m DB to 50%: (1) price hits a higher timeframe FVG such as a daily or 4-hour FVG; (2) price sweeps the leg containing the 5M M signal (you don't even have to close, just sweep the low); (3) a 5m FVG forms which can act as the entry for the 5M setup. When all three occur, the odds go from 80% to 50%. At this point we can only take a setup INTO the five-minute FVG: take the DB retracement entry, but be in a safe trade (stop at break even, 50% closed) by the time we tap the five-minute FVG. Then price could push through it and create a new one minute FVG inside it (continuation of the 1m DB), or respect it, fail to create FVGs in the DB direction and form new FVGs in the opposite direction: confirmation a reversal is taking place. That's the battle of the fair value gaps: whatever prints in there is the higher probability.

Ideally, for the DB to continue, we don't sweep the M leg and we don't print a five-minute FVG. But if you hit a higher timeframe FVG, create a five-minute FVG and sweep the M leg, look for a sell setup from that FVG; a buy setup can still be taken into it if you're safe by the time you hit it.

Live example: M signal, the market kept dropping and gave a double break right into a 4-hour FVG (criterion one). The next five-minute FVG formed (criterion two). The market continued up until it swept out the leg containing the M signal. On the five minute we had an M, hit the 4-hour FVG, no higher timeframe DB; first presented FVG, tapped, one minute FVG after consolidation. Even though the lower timeframe had a double break, the market swept the leg with the M, came back, bounced off the midpoint and set up to go long. That's how a reversal happens: hit a higher timeframe FVG, sweep the leg with the M, leave a five-minute FVG. It's a reversal on the lower timeframe, but on the five minute it's really just an M with an FVG.

Two plays either way: on the lower timeframe DB, deep retracement, a sell setup taken INTO that first presented five-minute FVG (be safe by the time you skim it, because it could reverse, which is exactly what happened); or wait for the market to tap that FVG and look for a reversal setup to go long.
