Core Strategy Walkthrough (M / DB signals) — companion to the Signal Legend PDF

M signal. As soon as you get an M signal (there's an arrow on the chart showing which way the market wants to go), the market wants to go the opposite way: M down means market up, M up means market down. After an M to the downside (bullish setup) I'm looking for the FIRST five minute fair value gap to be created. Paint it with the box tool, wait until the market comes back and touches it, then immediately drop to the one minute and look for the same thing: the first one minute FVG created off of this. Paint it. As the market comes back and taps it, drop to the 30-second and look for the first presented 30-second FVG. Wait for the market to come back and touch it, then look for a closure over the series of candles that created the push down into the 30-second. Could be two, three, four, five candles. We want a nice clean V shape, three or four candles. Rarely clean, so just recognise: market touches, then closes over the candle that started the push down into the 30-second. That's the entry. Stop loss goes under the 30-second leg, the leg that contains the 30-second FVG; it's usually the candle that hit the one minute FVG, because that's where everything branched off. Use the position calculator for 1:3 and line the 3R up with a liquidity zone or a higher timeframe FVG. M up: exact same thing mirrored.

My directional bias doesn't change unless something changes it. If the market crashes through the 30-second FVG and comes back to the one minute, that 30-second is invalidated; delete it, bias unchanged, look for the next 30-second off the one minute. If it pushes past the one minute and comes back to the five minute, bias unchanged; look for a new one minute and a 30-second off that. Only when the market crashes through all three FVGs and takes out the low (the M leg) does it turn into a DB to the downside. Now my directional bias has changed and I'm looking for a sell.

DB. Our data shows an 80% chance the market retraces back into our zone. Then the same cascade: five minute, one minute, 30 seconds. The only thing that changed is there was a retracement first. What constitutes a retracement / how do you know it's done? If there's a fair value gap left in the leg that gave you the DB, we'd like the market to come back and touch it. Does it have to? No, but it tells me the market will give a decent retracement; otherwise it's a runaway and could keep creating FVGs and take off from us. The best entries happen under the double break: it comes back in and we use that to go out, because I want to be in a safe trade by the time I clear the DB high. That's all a DB means: a retracement and a push to take out that high. Ideally 2R is right around the DB high (rarely, but that's optimal); safe by there, let the rest ride to 3R.

The retracement is done when: (1) we get a pullback into the range and hit a FVG in the DB leg (the leg that created the double break, i.e. the crash that took out the M leg), or if there is no FVG, the market pulls back and maybe touches the midpoint of the double break candle; (2) an immediate rebalance: one candle up, one candle down, an engulfing candle; (3) a fair value gap created in the direction of the double break: the five minute FVG. Then the same cascade.

Triangle. We still respect the first M. A second M does not mean the market wants to sell off; it means a retracement is coming. The first M and the first DB are the important ones. After an M I get a five minute FVG, one minute, 30 seconds. The second M means a 70% chance the market retraces back and looks for that setup. If I'm already in a setup and get a second M, secure profits, roll stops, protect. If we didn't even hit the first five minute FVG and got a second M, that's a good sign: the market wants to come back and touch it; that's where I look for my long. If it then turns into a DB in the original direction, still good: M said up, DB says up, the second M just means a retracement. The DB only goes up after a retracement: first five minute, one minute, 30 second entry, and be safe by the time we clear the DB.

2DB. If we then drop and it turns into a 2DB, we avoid it: too many directional biases, it's tricking us too many times. M, 2M, DB, and now 2DB: done. Those are the main signal setups. Then we layer blue zones, reversal setups, etc. on top of these core fundamentals.
