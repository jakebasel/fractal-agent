## 1. Session timing and trading windows
- Sessions begin where the indicator's boxes start: Asia 8pm ET, London 2am ET. NY has no box.
- Best NY setups usually come within 30-40 minutes of the open.
- Time within a session is NEVER a skip (amendments 2026-10-07, Jake: the whole session is
  tradeable; the items below are the instructor's time preference, he values his time):
  - "Two hour" remark: technically valid until the next session, odds fall after the first hour; he personally has not stayed past 10:30-11:00 ET.
  - No signals at all by 10:00-10:30 ET: he walks away (the delay is another layer of manipulation).
  - Sweet spot: in the trade by about 10:30-10:40 ET; later setups get a downgrade (reduced size).
  - Consolidation well after 10:30 ET is "a bad sign": downgrade.
  - Late-session setups: reduced size (he says "demo").
- Sloppy morning: skip it and check the PM session's signals.
- "Blue zone play": from 9:30 ET price is likely to pull back to the NY blue zone, especially if untouched. Still needs the full setup (1m FVG, 30s tap, close). Don't pre-empt.
- At the Asia open price is similarly drawn to the purple zone.

## 2. News, events and bad-condition days
- High-impact news days are do-not-trade days; never bend the strategy to fit them.
- CPI/PPI weeks: demo only.
- A DB printed on a news candle is void.
- After a surprise shock (e.g. a tweet wick): discard the old 1m FVG, wait for a new one, demand more confluence, demo only.
- Live speeches: untradeable while they talk; demo only.
- 2DB within about 20 minutes of the open = heavy-manipulation day; demote to paper.
- After-hours earnings (e.g. AMD) can move NAS.
- Day trading is not every-day trading: recognize bad conditions and don't jump into rapids.

## 3. Choosing which index to trade
- Watch 2-3 assets; one usually gives the clean setup. Watching one leads to forcing trades.
- Pick the one that retraces back into the range. Runaways become obsolete ("too hot, too cold, just right").
- The weaker pair dictates timing when indices disagree.
- Don't buy the weaker pair (weak pullbacks stop you out repeatedly): "great for a sell, horrible for a buy". Buy the stronger, sell the weaker.
- Trade the one "telling the most consistent story". Drop an index that whipsaws or won't leave FVGs.
- Majority rules: if two of three indices have a 5m DB up, he won't sell anything that day.
- Directional filter per index: a DB or Spotlight confirmation one way means that index can only be traded that way. An index with conflicting signals "disqualifies itself".
- A signal-less index can be the tiebreaker.
- Wedged index (stuck between a 4H FVG, the DSD and the prior day's high/low): skip, no room for reward.
- Prefer indices still inside the range.
- Futures vs CFD can show different FVGs/signals; patterns usually sweep the same liquidity.

## 4. Timeframe selection (1m play vs 5m play)
- Market Translator is designed for 15m, 5m, 1m. Signals print on the 30s but the fractal breaks down there; not designed for 1h or above (false readings).
- Stick to the signal's timeframe: a 5m DB needs a 5m retracement and 5m FVG; a 1m DB needs 1m ones.
- A 1m play can skip the 5m step: immediate rebalance, 1m FVG, 30s FVG, close.
- On a 5m DB pulling back into a blue/purple zone or the NY 5m opening gap, he may drop to the 1m (price often won't pass the zone and the 5m may never print an FVG).
- Fractal conflict: 1m DB one way plus a 5m single M with a 5m FVG. If a Spotlight confirmation prints in that direction it acts like a higher-timeframe DB: play the 5m setup.
- A fresh 5m FVG raises the odds; before a counter-trend sell he insists on one. Until a 5m FVG forms, back-and-forth in the range is just rebalancing.
- Watch the 1m and 5m together. If a 5m DB prints against your 1m trade, roll the stop or close.

## 5. Signal configurations
- 2M: about 70% chance price returns to the first presented 5m FVG between the Ms.
- True triangle M, M, DB: returns to the FVG between the Ms, then heads in the DB direction.
- False triangle M, DB, M: no information; stick with the DB; can quickly become a 2DB.
- 2DB voids the setup and that index for the rest of the session ("not interested in trading" it). Never traded (amendments). A 2DB within ~20 minutes of the open = heavy-manipulation day: paper only.
- The DB is the "dealer revealing its hand": once a definitive DB or Spotlight confirmation is in, don't fight it. Bias flips only with full confluence.
- After a DB he stops looking for reversal setups (older live rule; the Reversal Set Up video now gives a reduced-size reversal model).
- A DB with no retracement is a runaway: no foothold, no trade.
- On a DB, no counter sell until the leg is swept; then look for a sell off a 5m FVG. You can take the DB-direction buy if you are safe by the time price reaches that FVG.
- The move is done once higher-timeframe liquidity (e.g. prior day's high) or a higher-timeframe FVG is taken: "it's a buy model until it's not".
- A single M on the higher timeframe means the fractal is not complete.
- London/Asia obligation: an unsatisfied prior-session signal is confluence and a take-profit/stop-roll level, never a trigger.
- He prefers continuation over reversal. With bearish confirmations and DBs on all three indices it is "a sell or nothing".
- Reversals usually leave an FVG to reverse from; tight wick-to-wick candles with no FVG are suspicious.
- If price sweeps or closes beyond both the M leg and the DB leg, a reversal may be underway: reassess.

## 6. DB retracement rules
- A DB needs a retracement back into the range before entry.
- Retracement zone: any FVG inside the DB leg, plus an FVG in the one candle to the right of the DB candle. Anything above that is ignored (power of three can use it and take you out).
- If the DB leg has no FVG, price must at least close back beyond the high/low of the DB candle; even that is only a short retracement.
- Match the timeframe: a 1m play needs a 1m FVG in the DB leg; a 5m play a 5m FVG.
- A retracement to an earlier FVG is "deep enough"; too shallow means skip.
- Preferred pattern: DB, deep retracement to a purple zone, immediate rebalance, 5m FVG.

## 7. Fair value gaps
- Invalidation: a candle on the same timeframe closes beyond the high/low of the leg containing the FVG and the next candle does not rebalance back in. A wick, or a close followed by an immediate rebalance, is a stop hunt: still valid.
- Leg: in a down leg each candle takes out the prior low without taking the prior high (up legs mirrored). Wicks count; inside bars don't. A break must be at least one tick beyond.
- First presented FVG: ~70%+ chance of being tapped; the market often comes back for it.
- He never wavers on the 5m (first presented only); tries for first presented 1m; more lenient on 30s (first two if they form a string, never a third).
- One compromise per setup; everything else first presented.
- Avoid a second 5m FVG; don't count a 5m FVG formed after heavy consolidation.
- Once a 4H/daily FVG is tapped, earlier ones are deleted; the tapped one is the reversal reference.
- Bias tie-break: which direction is printing more 5m FVGs.
- An FVG ripped through two or three times signals consolidation: stay out.
- The NY 5m opening gap counts like a blue zone.
- Don't use the inverse of the Spotlight opening gap as the only confluence.

## 8. Entry
- Enter only on a body close beyond the line over the candles that tapped the 30s FVG.
- Wait for every step; if it runs without you, let it go. Losses taken following every rule are a business expense.
- Never chase.
- Reject poor R:R (about 1:1).
- Don't enter long deeper into an overhead 4H FVG.
- Best moves go from inside the range to outside it; outside-the-range starts get reduced size.
- "Juice already drunk": require the full setup on both indices and use a reduced lot.
- A 30s FVG far from the tap leaves poor R:R.
- Tight wick-to-wick candles = "they don't want you in".
- Don't anticipate a move into a zone without a real setup.

## 9. Stops
- Stop at the low (long) / high (short) of the leg containing the 30s FVG. One stop only.
- Don't widen stops.
- Before an expected higher-timeframe DB into all-time highs, secure a partial or move the stop to the midpoint.

## 10. Management
- 1:3 target; 50% off and breakeven at 2R; align 2R with a liquidity level when possible (1.96R is fine).
- Purple/blue zone ahead: roll and take 50% there even short of 2R.
- Under 2R you may roll to breakeven without closing anything ("freebie").
- Aim for candle-body highs, not the DSD high itself.
- Be safe before the next obstacle (DB high, prior leg high, zone).
- If price reaches a 4H/daily FVG or HTF liquidity and consolidates there before the trade is safe, exit at breakeven. (The "10-15 minutes" version is unverified.)
- Exit at breakeven if price breaks a protected low after power of three already happened.
- All-time highs have no liquidity above: a DB there can simply drop; roll early.
- Below the 238 EMA price tends toward the NDOG.
- After a big drop the market often pulls back to sweep late sellers.

## 11. Consolidation
- 5+ candles at the 30s FVG = power-of-three risk: wait one more candle to close outside the range, safer stop at candle 1 of the 30s FVG. Price ripping through the same FVG a third time: stay out. ("5-7 candles then divergence only" is unverified.)
- Heavy back-and-forth at an FVG and a zone at once is a trap.
- The higher probability is off the larger structure.

## 12. Zones and weekly context
- Thursdays like to retrace to the weekly range (aqua lines).
- Deepest purple (maroon) is the strongest zone; watch overlaps (blue zone start at a purple midpoint).
- A fresh purple zone printed during a DB run warns price may not pass it.
- Price commonly retraces to purple zones and the NDOG.
- 20 EMA retracements are fine; a 20-under-50 cross across all timeframes is a bias he won't trade against.
- An index drifting between a 4H FVG and a higher-timeframe wick without taking liquidity is not a setup.

## 13. Spotlight
- Spotlight confirmation is about 80% accurate; don't fight it.
- A confirmation that never breaks out, with no MT signal late in the session, is weak.
- A confirmation one way blocks that index's opposite trades.
- A confirmation still needs a retracement (e.g. to a blue zone) before entry.
- The instructor will take a Spotlight setup alone (correction to older notes that said it needs an MT signal).

## 14. Asia / evening
- At the Asia open price is drawn to the purple zone; watch the NDOG for a reaction.
- Pre-news nights are practice only.
- A DB straight into a 4H FVG: expect a retracement or reversal; drop the index if it closes back below.

## 15. Mindset

- A directional bias is only one part; entry, R:R and risk management matter as much.
